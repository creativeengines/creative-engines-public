# Buyability

How ready a brand is to be *bought*: recognised, trusted, and easy to say yes to.

Buyability is the B2B marketing industry's term, established by LinkedIn's B2B Institute with Bain &
Company and carried since by the major industry bodies. Creative Engines works one level down from
where that research stops: it reads buyability **on a specific message, before the money moves.**

## Why the idea matters

Marketing is usually judged after the fact. A message goes out, the numbers come back weeks later,
and by then the budget behind it is spent. Buyability names the property that decides those numbers
before they exist: whether the people this message is for find the brand credible enough, relevant
enough, and clear enough to act on.

That property is not a matter of taste. It can be read – and a message that reads badly can be fixed
while fixing is still free.

## How it relates to the Reason to Buy

They are the same idea from two sides. The [Reason to Buy](./reason-to-buy.md) is the **asset a brand
builds** – the specific reason its buyers choose it, surfaced from their own words and pressure-tested
before spend. Buyability is the **condition that reason produces** – what a brand gains once the
reason is found, proven, and carried consistently everywhere it speaks.

A brand raises its buyability by improving the reason, not by talking about buyability.

## What Creative Engines adds

The industry frame describes buyability as a property of a brand. Two Creative Engines terms extend
it into something a marketer can act on this afternoon, at the level of the individual piece of work.

### Buyability Preflight

**Buyability Preflight** is the Creative Engines term for putting a message in front of
[synthetic Mindsets](./test-before-you-publish.md) – buyer models built from real buyer evidence,
never real respondents – **before it ships and before money is committed to it.** It returns where
the message loses people and what to change, and a person approves what survived.

Each Mindset reads the message **separately and in isolation**. That is deliberate: a single averaged
verdict hides the thing worth knowing. A message often works for one kind of buyer and quietly fails
another, and a marketer needs to see *which* one, not a blended number.

The name borrows the sense a preflight check has in aviation and print production: the inspection that
happens **before the run**, not the post-mortem after it.

### Buyability Index

**Buyability Index** is the Creative Engines term for what a Preflight hands back: a composite result
with a band, broken down by Mindset so a marketer can see which buyer a message loses, comparable
between versions of the same message.

Three limits are part of the definition, not caveats attached to it:

1. **It reads a message, not a brand.** The Index reflects how one piece of work reads to modelled
   buyers. A brand's overall buyability is built from far more than any message – how widely it is
   known, who vouches for it, how risky it feels to adopt – and no message test observes that.
2. **It is a modelled judgement, not observed behaviour.** The Index comes from synthetic Mindsets.
   No real conversion, click, or deal data is involved, and it is never a forecast of response,
   conversion, or revenue. It is comparative: this version read better than that one, to these
   modelled buyers.
3. **It is a way to compare, not a certificate.** A higher Index means a message survived more
   scrutiny than its alternatives. It does not promise a market result, and Creative Engines does not
   offer one.

## What this changes in practice

The useful shift is *when* the question gets asked. Most teams learn whether a message was buyable
after the campaign has run and the budget is gone. A Preflight moves that question in front of the
spend, where the answer is still cheap to change.
