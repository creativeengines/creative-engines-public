# Buyability

How ready a brand is to be *bought*: trusted, credible, and easy for a whole buying group to say yes
to – and to defend afterwards.

## Where the word comes from

Buyability is not a Creative Engines coinage. It is the term the B2B marketing industry adopted from
the research programme LinkedIn's B2B Institute ran with Bain & Company, presented publicly from 2025
and carried since by the major industry bodies. Creative Engines uses the industry's word rather than
inventing a private one, extends it toward the work of actually improving the thing it names, and does
not claim to have originated it.

## Why the idea matters

Buyers rarely choose alone. A purchase of any size is agreed by a group – the person who wants it,
the people who must live with it, the person who signs – and each of them carries a different risk.
So the option that wins is often not the one that is best on paper, but the one that is **easiest to
agree on and safest to defend later**. That is why deals stall into no decision rather than into a
loss to a competitor: not because the case was wrong, but because the group could not converge on it.

Buyability names that property. A brand with high buyability is one the group already recognises,
trusts, and can point at without anyone having to justify the choice from scratch.

## How it relates to the Reason to Buy

They are the same idea from two sides. The [Reason to Buy](./reason-to-buy.md) is the **asset a brand
builds** – the specific reason its buyers choose it, surfaced from their own words and pressure-tested
before spend. Buyability is the **condition that reason produces** – what a brand gains once that
reason is found, proven, and carried consistently everywhere it speaks.

A brand raises its buyability by improving the reason, not by talking about buyability.

## Buyability Preflight – the check you run

**Buyability Preflight** is the Creative Engines name for putting a message in front of
[synthetic Mindsets](./test-before-you-publish.md) – buyer models built from real buyer evidence,
never real respondents – *before* it ships and before money is committed to it. It returns where the
message loses people and what to change, and a person approves what survived.

The name borrows the sense a preflight check has in aviation and in print production: the inspection
that happens **before the run**, not the post-mortem after it.

## Buyability Index – the reading it returns

**Buyability Index** is the Creative Engines name for what a Preflight hands back: a composite reading
with a band and a per-Mindset breakdown of where a message loses people, comparable between versions
of the same message.

Three limits are part of the definition, not caveats attached to it:

1. **It reads the message, not the brand.** The Index reflects how much a specific message
   contributes to buyability. It is not a measurement of a brand's total buyability – the industry
   construct is relational (how widely a brand is known across a buying group, whether peers vouch for
   it, how risky adoption feels, how defensible the choice is later), and no message test can observe
   most of that.
2. **It is a modelled judgement, not observed behaviour.** The reading comes from synthetic Mindsets.
   No real conversion, click, or deal data is involved, and it is never a forecast of response,
   conversion, or revenue. It is comparative: this version read better than that one, to these
   modelled buyers.
3. **It is a way to compare, not a certificate.** A higher reading means a message survived more
   scrutiny than its alternatives. It does not promise a market result, and Creative Engines does not
   offer one.

## What this changes in practice

The useful shift is *when* the question gets asked. Most teams learn whether a message was
buyable after the campaign has run and the budget is gone. A Preflight moves that question in front of
the spend, where it is still cheap to change the answer.
