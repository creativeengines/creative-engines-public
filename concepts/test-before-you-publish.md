# Test before you publish

This is the **Test** step of Creative Engines' three-step plan, and the conviction under it is simple: a message should be put in front of the people it is for before it ships, not after it underperforms.

## The cost of shipping blind

Most content goes out untested. A piece that misses burns the spend quietly; a piece that misreads the room can turn into a public misstep. Either way the team finds out only after the audience already has, when the fix is expensive and the impression is made.

Testing has always been the answer, and it has always been the step that gets cut. Real audience research is slow, expensive, and arrives after the decision has been made, so in practice a launch rides on whoever argued best in the room.

## What a synthetic Mindset is

A **synthetic Mindset** is a buyer model built from real public buyer evidence: what people in that position say they are trying to do, what they believe, what they fear, and what would have to be true before they act.

The word *synthetic* is doing deliberate work. These are models, never real customers, survey respondents, or interviewees, and nothing they produce is a market result. Naming that plainly is what makes the step honest, and it is why the human approval gate is never removed: a person decides what survived.

The Mindsets that react to the work are the same ones that shaped it upstream, which is why the reaction is specific to the intended audience rather than a generic readability check.

## What the test asks

Before a piece is approved, it can be put in front of those Mindsets and asked what a real panel would be asked:

- What did you understand this to be saying?
- What did you feel reading it?
- What was unclear, or made you stop?
- What would you need to see before you believed it?
- Have you heard this before from someone else?

That last question matters more than it looks. Sameness is not a style problem – it is the reason a category stops being able to charge a premium – and it is easiest to catch in the reaction of someone who has already read nine versions of it.

## The Buyability Index

The check is a **Buyability Preflight**, and what it returns is a **Buyability Index**: how convincing the message was, broken down by Mindset.

The reading is comparative. It exists to help a team choose between candidate messages – this angle held up, that one did not – and to show where a claim needs proof it does not yet have. It is never a prediction of response, conversion, click-through, or revenue, and it should never be quoted as one. A model can tell you a message landed flat with the buyer-type it was written for. It cannot tell you what the market will do.

## Why it matters

Catching a weak or risky piece before launch is the difference between a quiet edit and a public one. It turns publishing from a gamble into a rehearsed decision, and it moves the argument from taste to evidence: instead of the most senior opinion in the room deciding which message ships, the message that survived contact with the buyer does.

It also changes what approval means. A person still signs off on everything that represents the brand – but they sign off on work that has already been challenged, rather than on a first guess.

## Public boundary

This page explains a product philosophy and what a reader gets. It does not describe how synthetic Mindsets are constructed, what evidence sources they draw on, how a panel is assembled or run, or how a Buyability Index is produced, scaled, or weighted.
