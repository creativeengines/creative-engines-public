# Source Grounding and Search

Source Grounding and Search is the capability that helps Creative Engines use approved brand material when strategy or content work needs context.

The client-facing idea is straightforward: the product should not invent the brand from memory. It should retrieve relevant source material and keep work grounded in what the brand actually knows, says, and can support.

## What the user sees

A user can organize source material for a brand, such as:

- Brand documents.
- Strategy decks.
- Product or service notes.
- Transcripts and internal explanations.
- Web exports and approved public material.
- Examples of strong or weak content.

When downstream work needs context, Creative Engines can use source grounding to bring relevant passages or ideas into the task.

## How search helps

Creative Engines can use hybrid search to combine semantic relevance with keyword relevance. At a client level, this means the product can find material that is conceptually related to the task, not only material that repeats the exact same words.

This matters because brand knowledge is often scattered across decks, notes, interviews, pages, and prior work. Good content needs the right context at the right time.

## What it helps decide

Source Grounding and Search helps answer:

- What source material supports this claim or idea?
- What context should constrain this draft?
- What should the content system know before writing?
- Which approved material is missing or outdated?
- Where does the product need more context before producing work?

## How it connects to the product

Source grounding supports [Brand Strategy](../brand-strategy/README.md), [Mindset Intelligence](../mindset-intelligence/README.md), and [Creative Studio](../creative-studio/README.md).

It also supports [Editorial Governance](../../concepts/editorial-governance.md), because reviewers can ask whether content decisions are grounded in approved context.

## Public boundary

This page can name hybrid search, Python, and LLM calls at a high level. It does not publish retrieval settings, source-processing rules, quality evaluation details, storage design, or private knowledge workflows.
