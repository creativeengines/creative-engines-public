# Content Distribution

Content Distribution is the product area for turning content from isolated drafts into managed work: content pillars and campaigns, calendars, channels, tasks, review states, and reuse.

This is where Creative Engines connects production to an operating rhythm.

## What is the difference between a Content Pillar and an Ad Campaign?

Creative Engines organizes content into two kinds of motion:

- **Content Pillars** are ongoing, recurring themes a brand publishes against continuously – the steady editorial rhythm that builds a position over time. A pillar answers what a brand stands for, week after week.
- **Ad Campaigns** are time-bound motions organized around a specific objective, launch, or offer, with a defined start and end. A campaign answers what the brand is pushing right now, and until when.

Both draw on the same approved brand context and produce content assets the team can review and schedule. Content then moves as **tasks** through named, auditable stages, from a draft to a scheduled, published piece.

## What it does today

- **The content grid** turns recurring content themes into a plan – which theme runs on which channel, in which format, at what weekly cadence.
- **Content Pillars and Ad Campaigns** organize content into recurring and time-bound motions tied to the grid.
- **A content calendar** with a production view (grouped by theme, dated by deadline) and a publishing view (grouped by channel, dated by scheduled time), with status for planned, drafted, approved, and published work.
- **Channel setup** defines the channels a brand publishes to.
- **Scheduling and publishing** – attach an approved draft to a dated slot, and push approved content to channels automatically through native CMS connectors (Webflow, WordPress, Ghost) and a social aggregator, with publish status tracked back.
- **A pre-publication review and approval gate** that halts before anything ships and waits for an explicit human approval, or a no-go with notes.
- **The content task lifecycle** – every task moving through named, auditable stages with an explicit state, so work can be tracked and sent back a stage with notes.
- **Per-stage execution modes** – choosing how much of each stage runs automatically versus needs a human.
- **Multilingual content** – a default brand language with per-channel overrides.
- **Draft-ready notifications** – alerting the right operator when approved content is ready to publish.

## On the roadmap

- **Advanced distribution motions** – cross-channel orchestration, outreach personalization, pre-call research and post-call summaries, reactive PR press-hook drafting, influencer relationship-building, niche-forum engagement, and product-launch-event orchestration.

## What it helps decide

- Which content work belongs together, as a recurring pillar or a time-bound campaign.
- Which audience or Mindset each task serves, and which narrative it supports.
- Which channel or format fits the asset.
- What should happen before a draft is considered ready.
- How a piece should be reused instead of forgotten.

## Public boundary

This page explains content operations at a client level. Creative Engines produces and organizes the content for a pillar or campaign; it does not buy media. It does not publish connector details, private workflow logic, internal state-machine design, automation internals, or distribution mechanics that are not ready for public documentation.
