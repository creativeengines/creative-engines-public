# Market Intelligence

Market Intelligence is the product area for understanding the outside context around a brand: competitors, category movement, and the signals that should inform strategy before content is produced.

The purpose is to keep a team from planning content in a vacuum.

## What it does today

- **A competitor snapshot inside the AI-visibility audit** – when a brand runs the audit, the result includes a short competitor view: the leading competitor by AI-assistant mentions, plus a small table of rival domains with backlink strength and authority signals. It is a first read on who a brand is up against in AI search and link authority, with no manual research.

## On the roadmap

- **A competitor radar** – adding named competitors per brand and tracking them over time: profile cards (domains, channels, posting cadence, key topics), an activity timeline, and benchmarking against the brand's own activity. Repeatable, structured benchmarking instead of ad-hoc research.
- **A market and category-trend radar** – watching category-level demand, emerging topics, and trend signals that are not tied to a single named rival, and surfacing them to strategy, so a team can see where the category is heading before it shows up in their own numbers.

## What it helps decide

- Which narratives are worth defending.
- Which category assumptions should be challenged.
- Which buyer objections need better explanation.
- Which content themes are crowded and which are still open.
- Where the brand should sound different from generic category language.

## How it connects to other product areas

Market context feeds [Brand Strategy](./brand-strategy.md), because positioning and go-to-market planning should reflect what the market is already hearing. It also informs [Mindset Intelligence](./mindset-intelligence.md), because buyer beliefs are shaped by the claims and expectations buyers meet before they meet the brand. When a team moves into [Creative Studio](./creative-studio.md), market context helps content avoid repeating what every other brand in the category already says.

## Public boundary

This page explains the public product purpose. It does not publish source-selection rules, monitoring mechanics, internal research procedures, scoring models, or protected market-analysis methods.
